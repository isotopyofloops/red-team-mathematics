A study of the quasi covering dimension of finite lattices

D. Boyadzhiev1 · D. Georgiou2 · A. Megaritis3 · F. Sereti2

Received: 4 November 2018 / Revised: 25 March 2019 / Accepted: 13 May 2019 / Published online: 22 May 2019

Abstract
The notion of dimension for posets has been studied extensively (see, for example, Bae in
J Korean Math Soc 36(1):15–36, 1999, Dube et al. in Discr Math 338:1096–1110, 2015,
Gierz et al. in A compendium of continuous lattices. Springer, Berlin, 1980, Trotter in Com-
binatorics and partially ordered sets: dimension theory. Johns Hopkins University Press,
Baltimore, 1992, Vinokurov in Soviet Math Dokl 168(3):663–666, 1966, Zhang et al. in
Discr Math 340(5):1086–1091, 2017). Moreover, in the view of matrix theory, some of
these dimensions, such as the order dimension, the Krull dimension and the covering dimen-
sion, have been studied using the so-called order and incidence matrices (see Boyadzhiev
et al. in Appl Math Comput 333:276–285, 2018, Dube et al. in Filomat 31(10):2901–2915,
2017, Georgiou et al. in Quaest Math 39(6):797–814, 2016). In this paper, we define a new
dimension for finite lattices, called the quasi covering dimension, and we study many of its
properties. We characterize it using matrices and we present an algorithm for computing this
dimension.

Keywords Quasi covering dimension · Quasi cover · Finite lattice · Dense element ·
Matrix theory

1 Introduction

The dimension theory of frames (especially, their covering dimension, their small inductive
dimension and their large inductive dimension) attracted the interest of many researchers
such as Charalambous, Gilmour, Banaschewski, Brijlall, Baboolal (see Banaschewski and
Gilmour 1989; Brijlall and Baboolal 2008, 2010; Charalambous 1974).
    Recently, this theory was enriched by the study in Georgiou et al. (2019), where a new
dimension for frames, which is called the quasi covering dimension, was defined and many
of its properties were investigated.
    In this paper, we develop a theory for the quasi covering dimension of finite lattices,
proving many results.
    Moreover, it is known that finite posets and studies of their dimensions have important
roles in discrete mathematics. This role is based on matrix algebra and algorithms (see Yáñez
and Montero 1999). For example, the order dimension, the Krull dimension and the covering
dimension have been studied using matrices (see Boyadzhiev et al. 2018; Dube et al. 2017;
Georgiou et al. 2016).
    Therefore, we complete our paper by studying the quasi covering dimension of finite
lattices in the view of matrix algebra. Both order matrices and incidence matrices play
important roles in this study.
    Especially, in Sect. 2, we remind basic definitions and notations from dimension theory of
finite lattices and matrix theory. In Sect. 3, we define the quasi covering dimension for finite
lattices and in Sect. 4, we compare several of its properties. In Sect. 5, since the meaning
of the dense element of an arbitrary finite lattice will play an essential role in this study, we
characterize it in terms of incidence matrices. Finally, in Sect. 6, we present an algorithm for
computing the quasi covering dimension of an arbitrary finite lattice.


2 Preliminaries

In this section we recall some definitions and notations that are needed in the rest of our
study. We mention that in what follows, by X = {x 1 , . . . , xn } we mean a finite lattice (X , ⩽)
for which x1 = 0 X and xn = 1 X are the least and the greatest element of X , respectively.
Additionally, for an element x of X , we use the notation of ↓x = {y ∈ X : y ⩽ x}.

Definition 1 Let X be a finite lattice. A subset C of X is called a cover of X if C = xn
and x1 ∈/ C. A cover D of X is called a refinement of a cover C of X if for each d ∈ D there
exists c ∈ C such that d ⩽ c.

Definition 2 Let X be a finite lattice. The order of a subset C of X , ord(C), is defined to be
k, where k ∈ {0, 1, . . .}, if and only if the infimum of any k + 2 distinct elements of C is x 1
and there exist k + 1 distinct elements of C whose infimum is not x 1 .

Definition 3 The function dim, called covering dimension, with domain the class of all finite
lattices and range the set {0, 1, . . .}, is defined as follows:

(1) dim(X ) ≤ k, where k ∈ {0, 1, . . .}, if and only if for every cover C of X , there exists a
    cover R of X such that R is a refinement of C and ord(R) ≤ k.
(2) dim(X ) = k, where k ∈ {1, 2, . . .}, if dim(X ) ≤ k and dim(X )  k − 1.

3 The quasi covering dimension of finite lattices

In this section, we define the quasi covering dimension of an arbitrary finite lattice and we
present basic of its properties.
Definition 6 (Gierz
                et al. 1980) Let X be a finite lattice. A subset C of X is called a quasi
cover of X if C = x, where x is a dense element of X (that is, x ∧ y  = 0 X , for every
y ∈ X \{0 X }).
               Two quasi
                      covers C1 and C2 of X are called similar (in short, we write
C1 ∼ C2 ) if C1 = C2 . A quasi cover D of X is called a refinement of a quasi cover C
of X if for each d ∈ D there exists c ∈ C such that d ⩽ c.

Definition 7 The function dimq , called quasi covering dimension, with domain the class of
all finite lattices and range the set {0, 1, . . .}, is defined as follows:
(1) dimq (X ) ≤ k, where k ∈ {0, 1, . . .}, if and only if for every quasi cover C of X , there
    exists a quasi cover R of X such that C ∼ R, R is a refinement of C and ord(R) ≤ k.
(2) dimq (X ) = k, where k ∈ {1, 2, . . .}, if dimq (X ) ≤ k and dimq (X )  k − 1.

4 Properties of the quasi covering dimension of finite lattices

In this section, we study certain properties of the quasi covering dimension of different finite
lattices. Especially, having two finite lattices, we study the quasi covering dimension of their
linear sum, Cartesian product, lexicographic product and finally, of their rectangular product.

relation ⩽ is defined as follows:
                                    ⎧

                                    ⎩
                                      x ∈ X, y ∈ Y .
Clearly, if (X , ⩽1 ) and (Y , ⩽2 ) are finite lattices, then (X ⊕ Y , ⩽) is also a finite lattice.

Proof Is an immediate consequence of the following:

                  (a, b) ∈ ↓(x, y) ⇔ (a, b) ⩽ (x, y) ⇔ a ⩽1 x and b ⩽2 y
                                        ⇔ a ∈ ↓x and b ∈ ↓y ⇔ (a, b) ∈ ↓x × ↓y.

Lemma 3 Let (x, y) ∈ X × Y . Then, (x, y) is a dense element of X × Y if and only if x and
y are dense elements of X and Y , respectively.

Proof First we observe that for every (x1 , y1 ), (x2 , y2 ) ∈ X × Y we have
 (x1 , y1 ) ∧ (x2 , y2 ) = (x1 ∧ x2 , y1 ∧ y2 ) and (x1 , y1 ) ∨ (x2 , y2 ) = (x1 ∨ x2 , y1 ∨ y2 ).
Suppose that (x, y) is a dense element of X × Y and we prove that x is a dense element of
X . Let x  ∈ X \{0 X }. We consider the element (x  , 0Y ) of X × Y . Then, (x  , 0Y )  = 0 X ×Y
and since (x, y) is a dense element of X × Y ,
           (x, y) ∧ (x  , 0Y )  = 0 X ×Y ⇔ (x ∧ x  , y ∧ 0Y )  = (0 X , 0Y )
                                           ⇔ (x ∧ x  , 0Y )  = (0 X , 0Y ) ⇔ x ∧ x   = 0 X .
Similarly we can prove that y is a dense element of Y .
   Conversely, suppose that x and y are dense elements of X and Y , respectively. Let (x  , y  ) ∈
X × Y \{0 X ×Y }. Then, x   = 0 X or y   = 0Y . Therefore, x ∧ x   = 0 X or y ∧ y   = 0Y . Thus,
(x, y) ∧ (x  , y  ) = (x ∧ x  , y ∧ y  )  = 0 X ×Y and the proof of the lemma is complete.

Proposition 6 Let X and Y be finite lattices. Then,
                            dimq (X × Y ) = max{dimq (X ), dimq (Y )}.

Proof By Proposition 1 we have
        dimq (X × Y ) = max{dim(↓(x, y)) : (x, y) is a dense element in X × Y }.
By Lemmas 2 and 3 we have
           dimq (X × Y ) = max{dim(↓x × ↓y) : x and y are dense in X and Y }
and by Zhang et al. (2017) we have
      dimq (X × Y ) = max{max{dim(↓x), dim(↓y)} : x and y are dense in X and Y }.
Thus, adapting Proposition 1 for the quasi covering dimension of X and Y we get
                            dimq (X × Y ) = max{dimq (X ), dimq (Y )}.



Remark 5 (1) Let X and Y be finite lattices. Then, we obtain that dimq (X × Y ) = 0 if and
    only if dimq (X ) = dimq (Y ) = 0.
(2) If X and Y are finite lattices, then
                              dimq (X × Y ) < dimq (X ) + dimq (Y ) + 1.
(3) If X 1 , . . . , X n are finite lattices, then

Definition 13 (Dube et al. 2015) A cover C of X is called minimal if C ⊆ R, for every
refinement R of C. As we can see in Dube et al. (2015), every minimal cover is an antichain.

Conclusion

In this paper, we insert a new dimension for finite lattices, called the quasi covering dimen-
sion. We study many properties of this dimension and we characterize it using matrices. An
algorithm for computing the quasi covering dimension of an arbitrary finite lattice completes
our study.
