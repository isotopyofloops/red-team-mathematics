Abstract
Dimensions of partially ordered sets, lattices and frames have attracted the interest of various
researches (see for example Sancho de Salas and Sancho de Salas 1991; Vinokurov 1966;
Dushnik and Miller 1941; Hegde and Jain 2007; Trotter 1975; Boyadzhiev et al. 2018; Dube
et al. 2015, 2017; Georgiou et al. 2016; Hai-feng et al. 2017; Brijlall and Baboolal 2008,
2010; Boyadzhiev et al. 2019). For example, the order, Krull, covering dimension, and the
quasi covering dimension are some of these dimensions that have been studied extensively.
Especially, finite partially ordered sets and finite lattices are the main axes of this Dimension
Theory, investigating new results and characterizations for their dimensions. The matrix
algebra plays an essential role in these studies, considering incidence and order matrices.
Also, based on these researches, algorithms which compute these dimensions have been
investigated. Since the chapter on dimensions of partially ordered sets continues to attract the
interest, the small inductive dimension is a new notion that has been defined firstly for regular
frames in Brijlall and Baboolal (2008). In this paper, we study the small inductive dimension
for finite lattices using matrices. For that, we study the meaning of the pseudocomplement
and the lattice ↑x, where x is an element of a finite lattice. Based on these investigations we
present an algorithmic procedure for the matrix computation of the small inductive dimension
of an arbitrary finite lattice.

1 Introduction and preliminaries

The order, Krull, and covering dimension are dimensions that have been studied in the view
of matrix algebra for finite posets and finite lattices (see for example Boyadzhiev et al. 2018;
Dube et al. 2017; Georgiou et al. 2016). In Brijlall and Baboolal (2008, 2010) the notion of
the small inductive dimension for regular frames was defined and properties of this dimension
were investigated.
    In this paper, based on the notion of the small inductive dimension, we study this dimension
for finite lattices using matrices. Especially, using the order matrix and the incidence matrix,
we present an alternative approach to this dimension. An algorithmic procedure for the
computation of the small inductive dimension of an arbitrary finite lattice completes our
study.
    The motivation for this investigation follows by the continuous development of appli-
cations of Linear Algebra and Discrete Mathematics on pure theoretical computer science.
Many topological notions, and specifically topological dimensions, have been studied through
a discrete aspect. That is, combining classical topological notions with that of Linear Alge-
bra and Discrete Mathematics we can compute mathematical results by studying algorithmic
procedures.
    Such a study began from the investigation of finite spaces and Alexandroff spaces as topo-
logical models in Digital Topology to specify image analysis, image synthesis, computer
graphics and algorithms. More precisely, finite sets are one of the most important “keys" to
succeed in alternative investigations of topological notions since they have many applica-
tions for computer science, algorithms, programming languages, cryptography and software
development. This fact tends us to pay attention to the meaning of “finite". Partially ordered
sets and especially lattices are used also to investigate more properties of digital images and
their topological dimensions are the main axes of this investigation.
    Based on the above discussion, this paper is devoted to finite lattices, its kind of dimension,
the small inductive dimension, and a special investigation of this dimension. It is formed as
follows. In Sect. 2, we study the meaning of the pseudocomplement x ∗ of any element x
of arbitrary finite lattice L using matrices. An algorithm which finds this element gives a
different approach of this study. In Sect. 3, we study the finite lattice ↑(x ∨ x ∗ ), where x is an
arbitrary element of a finite lattice, presenting also an algorithm which determines the lattice
↑(x ∨ x ∗ ) through order and incidence matrices. Finally, in Sect. 4, we study the combination
of the small inductive dimension with the so-called minimal covers and present an algorithm
which computes the small inductive dimension of an arbitrary finite lattice using matrices.
    First of all, we recall basic definitions and notations which will be used in the following
sections.
    A set L endowed with a partial order ≤ is called a partially ordered set (usually, poset).
A poset L is called lattice if every finite subset of it has supremum and infimum in L.
    Moreover, two elements x and y of a poset (L, ≤) are said to be comparable if x ≤ y or
y ≤ x. A subset of a poset (L, ≤) in which every pair of elements is comparable is called a

chain. An antichain is a subset of a poset (L, ≤) in which any two distinct elements in the
subset are not comparable. If x and y are not comparable, we shall write x  y.
    We mention that in the rest of the study, we refer to a finite lattice L with n elements,
n ≥ 2. It will be denoted by L = {x 1 , . . . , xn }, where x1 = 0 L and xn = 1 L , are the bottom
and the top elements of L respectively.                                                  
    If L is a finite lattice and x ∈ L, then we write ↑x = {y ∈ L : x ≤ y} and x ∗ = {y ∈ L :
y∧x = 0 L }. In the case where x ∗ ∧x = 0 L , that is x ∗ = max{y ∈ L : y∧x = 0 L }, the element
x ∗ is called pseudocomplement of x. Every finite distributive lattice is pseudocomplemented.
Although the lattices we deal with in this paper are not generally distributive, we will adopt
the asterisk notation and the term “pseudocomplement" to have the same terminology as in
Brijlall and Baboolal (2008, 2010). We state that the poset ↑(x ∨ x ∗ ), where x ∈ L, is also
a finite lattice.                                                            
    A subset V of a finite lattice L is called a cover of L if 0 L ∈ / V and V = 1 L . A cover
U of a finite lattice L is called a refinement of a cover V of L, writing U          V , if for each
u ∈ U , there exists v ∈ V such that u ≤ v.
    Especially, a cover V of a finite lattice L is called minimal if V ⊆ U , for every refinement
U of V .
    The small inductive dimension was defined in Brijlall and Baboolal (2008) for regular
frames and properties of this dimensions have been studied in Brijlall and Baboolal (2008,
2010). Here we study the small inductive dimension for the class of finite lattices.
    Especially, if L is a finite lattice, then the small inductive dimension, ind, of L is defined
as follows: ind(L) ≤ k, where k ∈ {0, 1, . . .}, if for every cover V of L, there exists a cover
U of L such that U is a refinement of V and ind(↑(u ∗ ∨ u)) ≤ k − 1, for every u ∈ U . In
particular, ind(L) = k, where k ∈ {0, 1, . . .}, if ind(L) ≤ k and ind(L)  k − 1. Moreover,
we define ind(L) = −1 if and only if L = {0 L }.

This detailed separation of all possible relations is the reason for the use of this matrix. We
can use this matrix in order to succeed more results in Order Theory, enriching related studies.
For example, if ai j = 1, then we are sure that xi = x j and if ai j = 2, then certainly, xi < x j .
Thus, the combination of these two matrices gives us the desired results and characterizations
for the concepts of the paper.

Theorem 8 If (L, ≤) is a finite lattice, then we have that ind(L) ≤ k, where k ∈ {0, 1, . . .},

5 Discussion

In this paper, we study the small inductive dimension of finite lattices using matrix algebra.
Firstly, we investigate the notion of the pseudocomplement x ∗ of an element x of a finite
lattice through incidence and order matrices and present an algorithm which finds this ele-
ment. Then we study the lattice ↑(x ∨ x ∗ ) using order matrices and present an algorithm
which determines this lattice. Moreover, based on the notion of the minimal cover and the
matrix characterizations of the pseudocomplement and the lattice ↑(x ∨ x ∗ ), we provide an

algorithmic procedure which computes the small inductive dimension of an arbitrary finite
lattice.


6 Conclusion

In this paper, we present an alternative approach to the small inductive dimension for the
class of finite lattices using order and incidence matrices and algorithmic matrix procedures.
