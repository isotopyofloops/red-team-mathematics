Abstract
The Dimension Theory in the class of frames was the base of many researches. Especially,
the covering dimension, the quasi covering dimension, the small inductive dimension and the
large inductive dimension for frames have been studied extensively (see (Banaschewski and
Gilmour J. London Math. Soc. 39(2) 1–8 1989), (Brijlall and Baboolal Indian J. Pure Appl.
Math. 39(5), 375–402 2008), (Brijlall and Baboolal Indian J. Pure Appl. Math. 41(3), 535–553
2010), (Charalambous J. London Math. Soc. 8(2), 149–160 1974), (Charalambous Topology
Proc. 20, 49–66 1995), (Georgiou et al. Topology Appl. 275, 106995–13 2020)). Moreover,
the study of the meaning of dimension in Order and Lattice Theory tended to the study of
various dimensions, such as the order dimension for finite posets and the covering dimension
for finite lattices (see, for example, (Boyadzhiev et al. Appl. Math. Comput. 333, 276–285
2018), (Dube et al. Discrete Math. 338(7), 1096–1110 2015), (Georgiou et al. Quaest. Math.
39(6), 797–814 2016)). In this paper, we study the notion of the small inductive dimension,
ind, in the class of finite lattices. We provide a new characterization of this dimension, using
the so-called minimal covers, and investigate properties of this dimension. We study the small
inductive dimension of the linear sum, the Cartesian product, the lexicographic product and
the rectangular product of two finite lattices and the sublattice property. Also, we present
various examples, investigating the relation of the small inductive dimension with the Krull
and covering dimension.

1 Introduction

The Dimension Theory is a branch of Topology that has been studied extensively, defining
some of the most known dimensions, the covering dimension, the small inductive dimension
and the large inductive dimension (see, for example, [10–14]). Moreover, these dimensions
have been studied in the realm of finite and Alexandroff spaces (see, for example, [15–18]).
    However, many researchers such as M. Charalambous, G. Gilmour, B. Banaschweski, D.
Brijlall, D. Baboolal made a significant effort to develop a Frame Dimension Theory, defining
the covering dimension, the small inductive dimension and the large inductive dimension in
classes of frames (see [1–5]).
    Simultaneously, various dimensions have been studied in the realm of finite partially
ordered sets and lattices. The order dimension, the Krull dimension and the covering dimen-
sion are examples of such dimensions (see [7–9, 19, 20]).
    The term finite set is one of the main keys of Discrete Mathematics. Finite sets have many
important applications to computer science, algorithms, programming languages, cryptogra-
phy and software development. This fact tends us to pay attention to the meaning of finite. At
the same time, since Digital Topology studies the topological properties of digital images and
discrete arrays in two or more dimensions, the notions of dimensions, and more specifically
topological dimensions, win their own important role. Moreover, partially ordered sets and
especially lattices are used to investigate more properties of digital images (see for example
[21]).
    On the other hand, from a classical topological point of view, topological spaces like
spectral spaces have been studied under the prism of lattices [22]. This fact led to insert
definitions of topological notions in terms of lattices and more specifically finite lattices. The
meaning of dimension is one of these topological notions. As we have mentioned, the order
dimension, the Krull dimension and the covering dimension have been inserted and studied in
Lattice Theory. Therefore, a research issue that arises is the study of dimensions for lattices.
The combination of finite lattices and their dimensions provides new results which develop
the current research.
    In this paper, we study the notion of the small inductive dimension, ind, in the class of finite
lattices, proving characterizations and properties of this dimension. Especially, in Section 2,
we give a description of the small inductive dimension in details, giving a new characterization
of this dimension, using the so-called minimal covers and studying the sublattice property. In
Section 3, we study the small inductive dimension of the linear sum, the Cartesian product,
the lexicographic product and the rectangular product of two finite lattices and in Section 4,
we present various examples, investigating the difference between the small inductive dimen-
sion with the Krull and covering dimension.
    We remind, firstly, basic definitions and notations which will be used in order to define
the small inductive dimension. Throughout this paper we suppose that the latticesare finite.
    If L is a lattice and x ∈ L, then we write ↑x = {y ∈ L : x ⩽ y} and x ∗ = {y ∈ L :
y∧x = 0 L }. In the case where x ∗ ∧x = 0 L , that is x ∗ = max{y ∈ L : y∧x = 0 L }, the element
x ∗ is called pseudocomplement of x. Every finite distributive lattice is pseudocomplemented.
Although the lattices we deal with in this paper are not generally distributive, we will adopt

2 The Small Inductive Dimension for Finite Lattices

Based on the notion of the small inductive dimension, ind, which is defined for the class of
all regular frames [2], in this section, we study the small inductive dimension ind in the class
of finite lattices and we give properties of this dimension.
    We mention that we define this notion of dimension for the class of finite lattices because
we can see that this meaning is well defined and more friendly for this class. Clearly, we
can compute this dimension for regular finite lattices but with our study we realize that this
dimension behaves well for an arbitrary finite lattice.
    Since the dimension ind is defined inductively for finite lattices, we remind that a sublattice
of a lattice L is a subset of L, which is a lattice with the same meet and join operations as L.
Given a lattice L, every set of the form ↑x, where x ∈ L, is a sublattice of L.

Definition 1 Let L be a finite lattice. The small inductive dimension, ind, of L is defined as
follows:
1. ind(L) = −1 if and only if L = {0 L }.
2. ind(L) ⩽ k, where k ∈ N, if for every cover V of L, there exists a cover U of L such that
   U is a refinement of V and ind(↑(u ∗ ∨ u)) ⩽ k − 1, for every u ∈ U .
3. ind(L) = k, where k ∈ N, if ind(L) ⩽ k and ind(L)  k − 1.

   Analogously to Proposition 4.2 of [2], isomorphic lattices have the same small inductive
dimension.

3 Properties Concerning Sums and Products

In this section we study properties of the small inductive dimension of different finite lattices.
Especially, we study the small inductive dimension of the linear sum and three kinds of
products of finite lattices.

Proposition 22 For every finite lattice L, ind(L) < h(L).

Proof We prove the inequality by induction on h(L). Clearly if h(L) = 0, then L = {1 L }
and thus, ind(L) = −1, proving that the relation of the proposition holds.
   We suppose that the inequality holds for all finite lattices M with h(M) < k, k ⩾ 1, and
we shall prove it for k. Let L be a finite lattice with h(L) = k, V be a minimal cover of L and
v ∈ V . By Theorem 2 it suffices to prove that ind(↑(v ∨ v ∗ )) ⩽ k − 2. Since ↑(v ∨ v ∗ )  = L,
we have that h(↑(v ∨ v ∗ )) < h(L) and thus, h(↑(v ∨ v ∗ )) ⩽ k − 1. By inductive hypothesis
we have that ind(↑(v ∨v ∗ )) < k −1, proving that ind(L) ⩽ k −1 or equivalently ind(L) < k.

   A non-empty subset F of a lattice (L, ⩽) is called filter if F has the following properties:
  (i) F  = L,
 (ii) If x ∈ F and x ⩽ y, then y ∈ F,
(iii) If x, y ∈ F, then x ∧ y ∈ F.
A filter F is called prime if for every x, y ∈ L with x ∨ y ∈ F, we have x ∈ F or y ∈ F.
The set of all prime filters of a lattice L is usually denoted by PF (L).
   If PF (L)  = ∅, then the Krull dimension (see [26]) of L is defined as follows:
             Kdim(L) = sup{k : there exist prime filters F0 ⊂ F1 ⊂ · · · ⊂ Fk }.
Also, in the study [19], the Krull dimension is studied through join prime elements and
matrices. An element x ∈ L is said to be join prime if it is nonzero and the inequality
x ⩽ a ∨ b implies x ⩽ a or x ⩽ b, for all a, b ∈ L.
